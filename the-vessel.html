Spiritual vessels

The Aethelgard

A pentagonal resonance vessel, built as a sanctuary rather than a container. The Aethelgard brings sacred geometry and acoustic design together in one carved piece of tonewood, for families who want the vessel itself to carry meaning.

Sacred geometric architecture

Five sides, held to the golden ratio

The exterior is a five sided truncated pentagonal pyramid, bound throughout to the proportions of the golden ratio. In sacred geometry the pentagon stands for aether, the fifth element of spirit and cosmos, and the concentric rings carved into every panel carry that idea across the whole surface.

The panels are dense premium tonewood, three quarters of an inch thick, and they interlock without a single metal fastener. Nothing in the construction breaks the continuity of the vessel, either structurally or, in the maker’s intent, energetically.

The resonance cavity

A chamber shaped to hold sound

Inside, the vessel is precision carved into a smooth inverted ovoid dome, a shape drawn from the acoustic engineering of ancient megalithic temples. It is built as a Helmholtz resonance chamber, tuned to catch ambient vibration from the room around it and translate it into a continuous micro oscillation.

Where the outside is all straight facets and carved rings, the inside is a single uninterrupted curve. The two are doing quite different jobs.

The high density matrix

Quartz at the centre of it

The exterior shows the warm natural grain of the wood. The acoustic core within is something else entirely.

The chamber is lined with a proprietary matrix of 70 per cent crystalline quartz and 30 per cent polycast resin. The result is an ultra dense, glass smooth shield with close to zero internal damping, so sound waves reflect rather than dissipate and the chamber holds a sustained echo. Anchoring the centre is a solid, double terminated clear quartz pillar, there to stabilise the vessel’s internal frequency and keep what it holds in a settled harmonic alignment.
